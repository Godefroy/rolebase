    ## Org chart, RACI matrix or accountability chart?

    A classic org chart shows who reports to whom. A [RACI matrix](/en/blog/raci-matrix) lists who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed on each task of one project. An [accountability chart](/en/blog/accountability-chart) names, for each seat, the outcomes its holder owns. A growing company usually needs the third one first, because its reporting lines are known and its gaps sit in the work nobody clearly owns.

    Rolebase draws that accountability view as an org chart, and keeps it current as the team changes.

    | Question | Classic org chart | RACI matrix | Rolebase |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Who reports to whom? | Yes | No | Yes, in the hierarchical tree view |
    | What does each person own? | A job title | One project's tasks | Written accountabilities on every role |
    | Which work has no owner? | Hidden | Visible for one project | The vacant roles list |
    | Who keeps it up to date? | Whoever redraws it | The project lead | The team, live, in its meetings |

    Rolebase ships no RACI template. The rows of a RACI that read the same from one project to the next become accountabilities of a role, and the one-off work stays in tasks. Capterra gave Rolebase its Best Value and Best Ease of Use badges in 2023.
