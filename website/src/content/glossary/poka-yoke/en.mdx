Poka-yoke is a device or design choice that makes a mistake impossible, or catches it the moment it happens so it goes no further. The Japanese term translates as "mistake-proofing". Shigeo Shingo, an engineer who advised Toyota, made it part of the [Toyota Production System](/en/glossary/toyota-production-system) in the 1960s.

Shingo first used the word baka-yoke, "fool-proofing", and dropped it around 1963 because it insulted the workers. Poka-yoke means avoiding inadvertent errors. For Shingo, everyone makes slips when tired or interrupted, so the fix belongs in the design of the work rather than in blaming the operator.

## Two kinds of poka-yoke

A control poka-yoke stops the process until the error is corrected, like a machine that refuses to start while a part sits in the wrong position. A warning poka-yoke signals the error with a light or a sound and leaves the person to act, like the chime that sounds when a seat belt is unbuckled.

## Everyday examples

A keyed plug only fits one way. The cut corner of a SIM card prevents inserting it upside down. A microwave stops when its door opens. In hospitals, each medical gas has its own connector shape, so oxygen and another gas cannot be swapped by mistake. Online, a form that refuses a date in the wrong format works as a control poka-yoke.

A defect caught at its source costs less than one found at final inspection or by the customer. Lean counts defects among its seven wastes, or [muda](/en/glossary/muda). A poka-yoke also differs from a [checklist](/en/glossary/checklist): the checklist relies on someone reading it, while the poka-yoke keeps working when attention drops.
