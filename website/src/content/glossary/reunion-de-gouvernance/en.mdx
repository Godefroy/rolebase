A governance meeting is a meeting where a team changes its own structure: it creates, amends or removes roles and their accountabilities, sets the policies the team works by and elects people to elected roles. Day-to-day work goes to a separate [tactical meeting](/en/glossary/reunion-tactique). The format of a governance meeting depends on the governance model the organization follows, such as [holacracy](/en/glossary/holacratie) or [sociocracy](/en/glossary/sociocratie).

Companies, nonprofits and cooperatives hold governance meetings to set their policies and keep their structure in line with their objectives and values.

In holacracy, the [governance meeting process](/en/blog/governance-meeting) is written down in the Holacracy Constitution: three phases, six steps to process a proposal, and four criteria a concern has to meet before it counts as an objection.

A meeting runs faster when the people bringing a topic prepare it. They define the proposal clearly, gather the information it needs, say who presents each item and send the documents to participants beforehand.
