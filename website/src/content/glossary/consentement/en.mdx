Consent is a way of making group decisions in which a proposal is adopted as soon as nobody raises a reasoned objection to it. The question put to the group is whether anyone sees a reason the proposal would harm the group's work, which is a lower bar than getting everyone to agree.

## Consent and consensus

Consensus looks for everyone's support, so a single person who prefers another option keeps the discussion going. Consent moves the burden of proof to the person who blocks. They have to argue. A preference gets heard without stopping the decision. Sociocracy 3.0 sums up the test in one question: is the proposal good enough for now and safe enough to try? Groups usually give such a decision a review date, so they can adjust it once they have seen it work.

## What counts as an objection

An objection shows how the proposal would harm the work the group has committed to. "I would prefer something else" is a preference. A worry the person cannot yet back with reasoning is a concern, which gets recorded and blocks nothing.

[Holacracy](/en/glossary/holacratie) writes the test down. Its constitution treats a concern as an objection only when the objector argues four points:

- the proposal would reduce the circle's capacity to enact its purpose or accountabilities
- it would limit a role the objector represents in that circle
- the problem would be new, created by adopting the proposal
- the proposal would certainly cause that harm, or could cause it before the circle has a chance to adapt

The [facilitator](/en/glossary/facilitateur) checks that each point is argued, and leaves aside whether the argument is right.

## Where consent is used

[Sociocracy](/en/glossary/sociocratie) and holacracy use consent for governance decisions: creating or changing a [role](/en/glossary/role), setting a policy, electing someone to a role. Day-to-day decisions stay with the person who holds the role, inside its [domain](/en/glossary/domaine).
