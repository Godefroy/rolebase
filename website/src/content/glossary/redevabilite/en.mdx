Accountability is the obligation to answer for one's actions, decisions and results to someone entitled to ask: a manager, colleagues, shareholders, clients, citizens or a regulator. It goes further than [being responsible for a task](/en/blog/accountability-vs-responsibility). The responsible person does the work, while the accountable person has to explain what happened and bear the consequences, whether they acted alone or the decision was collective.

The word comes from "account": being accountable means having to give an account of what you did.

## Accountability in public life and organizations

Accountability applies to anyone who manages resources entrusted by others. Article 15 of the French Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 1789 states it for public administration: "Society has the right to require of every public agent an account of his administration." A charity answers to its donors for how it spends their money, a company to its shareholders for its results, an NGO to the people it serves.

Accountability covers financial management, regulatory compliance, the quality of work and ethical decisions. Organizations make it concrete through reports, audits, performance reviews and transparent communication. People can then see who answers for what, which builds trust. Errors, abuses and poor practices also come to light sooner.

## Accountability in role-based governance and in a RACI

The [accountabilities](/en/blog/define-roles) of a [role](/en/glossary/role) are the ongoing activities other roles can expect from it. A role also has a [purpose](/en/glossary/raison-detre), which says what it aims for, and a [domain](/en/glossary/domaine), which marks out what it decides alone.

Take a Communication role with two accountabilities: "answering press requests within 24 hours" and "publishing the monthly newsletter". If a journalist waits a week for an answer, the team knows whom to ask for an explanation. The person holding the role explains the delay and proposes a change, such as moving press requests to another role.

In a [RACI matrix](/en/blog/raci-matrix), which covers a single project, the A for Accountable names, for each deliverable, the one person who answers for its result.

In Rolebase, every role has an Accountabilities field that anyone on the team can read by opening the role from the org chart. You can [write the accountabilities of each role](/en/features) for free with up to five active members.
