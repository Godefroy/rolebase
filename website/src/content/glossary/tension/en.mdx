A tension, in [holacracy](/en/glossary/holacratie) and other forms of role-based governance, is a gap someone senses between how the organization works today and how it could work. The word is neutral. A tension signals something to improve, whether a problem or an opportunity. In holacracy, every change to roles or policies starts from one.

Tensions come from everyday work. A process takes three approvals for a routine purchase, two roles both think they own the newsletter, customer complaints sit unanswered because no role is in charge of them. Each of these is a tension. The person who feels a tension is best placed to raise it.

## Where tensions are processed

In holacracy, members look for tensions and bring each one to the meeting that handles it.

- A tension about roles, domains or policies goes to a [governance meeting](/en/blog/governance-meeting). The person who raised it turns it into a proposal, which the circle adopts by [consent](/en/blog/consent-decision).
- A tension about the work itself, such as a missing piece of information or a next step, goes to a [tactical meeting](/en/glossary/reunion-tactique).

When members process tensions this way, the organization changes in small steps, driven by the people who do the work, rather than all at once in a reorganization.
