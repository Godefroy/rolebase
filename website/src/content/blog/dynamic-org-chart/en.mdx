A dynamic org chart is one that the teams update themselves as their roles change, so it stays accurate without anyone redrawing it. Companies keep an org chart current in one of three ways: they redraw it by hand in a diagram tool, generate it from an HR system that tracks job titles and reporting lines, or let each team maintain its own roles in a tool like Rolebase. A tool synced with an HR system updates the chart when HR records a hire, a departure or a new manager. Rolebase stays separate from the HR system. The teams keep their roles and responsibilities current in it, and those change far more often than job titles do.

Self-organised businesses run on this kind of org chart. First, here is what you, as a founder or business owner, want in a high performing organisation:

1. To know YOUR place and where you bring the most value. This means not being sucked into day-to-day operations but focused on setting the vision, being the creative architect and perhaps face of the company, and playing up to your strengths while other people in your team play up to THEIR strengths. This demands clarity on role descriptions for everyone, including you !
2. The entire team to intimately know the company vision and current goals and priorities => you want to keep the team aligned so everyone is rowing in the same direction at all times.
3. At the same time, you want a team that's reactive and even proactive, a team that's able to move around quickly, that can swiftly add new responsibilities, remove old ones, shift people around, disband teams and recreate more relevant ones as the needs arise. You want to be able to set a clear vision, strategy and goals, but also leave the door open for adjustments along the way.
4. Those changes to be communicated instantly so people are acting from the same level of information, and not on outdated info.
5. Information to travel to the right place quickly. Typical information flow in a company tends to be up and down chains of command. This is ineffective and communicates often distorted information.
6. A business that acts on important signals quickly, however weak or strong the signal. This means giving people ownership of their work, i.e. defining and communicating clear decision making perimeters for every role and team.
7. With AI and more and more specialised tools, generalists are becoming more valuable than specialists. So you want a team that's able to be cross-functional, get the big picture quickly and implement wholesome solutions that bring together departments rather than pit them against one another.

A typical hierarchy doesn't satisfy these conditions, and we've written about why that is [​here​](/en/blog/hierarchy-problems).

## The living org chart

Self-organised teams have tackled these very questions by organising themselves not in a top-down or matrix-like way, but in **circles**.

This is inspired from the natural world with each cell, molecule, tissue, organ, being self-organised and self-contained while at the very same time being part of a bigger whole.

So it’s about creating autonomous teams that function symbiotically together to create something that's bigger than the sum of its parts.

## How does this org chart resolve the points above?

1. Teams, sub-teams and roles create**clarity**by expliciting:

- The purpose for why they exist
- Their decision-making perimeter (called domain)
- What they’re accountable for
- Any relevant checklist
- Key indicators they’re responsible for

- Self-managed businesses don't settle for ONE company vision, but allows teams, subteams and even roles to break down that bigger vision into their own sub-vision. This**aligns**the teams towards the common, bigger vision by bringing it closer to the teams and roles.
- The org chart is fully**dynamic**and allows teams and subteams to quickly add, remove or update information as and when they come up. This is done in a couple of clicks.
- Any information that’s been updated is**instantly available**for everyone to see, so people have access to the updated version at any given time.
- This level of clarity on who does what allows team members, especially new ones, to know immediately who to speak to for what. So information flows directly to the right people and helps new people to**onboard quickly**.
- You can define the 'Domain', or decision-making perimeter of each team, sub-team and roles. Rolebase also allows you to**track decisions**: what has been decided on, by whom, when, and in which context.
- In real life, we wear multiple hats: we’re parents, siblings, children, neighbours… Self-organised businesses allow people to have multiple roles across teams. They can thus show up in the places where they add value. This creates a team that understands multiple departments and can develop cross-functional capabilities. It also allows for**unseen work to be seen**by making it visible directly on the org chart.

## How do you keep an org chart up to date automatically?

An org chart stays up to date when the people whose work changes can edit it themselves, without waiting for HR or for whoever drew the chart. Companies use three approaches. Each one keeps a different part of the chart current.

| Approach | Example tools | What stays current | What it misses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drawn or imported in a diagram tool | Lucidchart, Miro, Creately | The last version someone drew, or the last spreadsheet someone imported | Every change made since then |
| Synced from an HR system or a directory | ChartHop, Pingboard, Organimi, OrgChart, Functionly, Ingentis | People, job titles and reporting lines, from the first sync after HR records the change | Responsibilities, decision rights and cross-team work, which someone has to enter by hand in the tool |
| Maintained by the teams, role by role | Rolebase | Roles, their accountabilities and decision rights, and the people holding each role, as soon as a team saves a change | Hires, departures, job titles and reporting lines, since Rolebase takes nothing from an HR system or directory |

A diagram tool works well for a one-off chart, such as a reorganisation plan or a board presentation. The chart goes stale as soon as the person who drew it moves on to other work. Lucidchart and Miro can also build a chart from a spreadsheet exported from the HR system. In Lucidchart, someone clicks a button to [refresh the chart from that file](https://help.lucid.co/hc/en-us/articles/16463330693268-Create-an-org-chart), while Miro needs the file imported again. Either way, the chart is only as recent as the last export.

An HR-generated chart updates itself when HR records a hire, a departure or a new manager. That is what most tools mean by an automatic org chart. ChartHop [syncs once a day](https://docs.charthop.com/sync-payroll-system) from the payroll or HR system. [OrgChart](https://theorgchart.com) (theorgchart.com) runs its sync daily, weekly or on demand. The chart still follows the employment contract, though, which changes less often than the work itself. A product manager who takes over the onboarding flow or a developer who joins the security working group changes no field in the HR system, so the sync never brings that change in. Functionly [refreshes its HR data every 24 hours](https://www.functionly.com/orginometry/integrations-keep-your-current-org-charts-up-to-date) and also stores roles and responsibilities next to positions. Someone edits those roles by hand in Functionly, since the HR system holds none of them.

In Rolebase, the teams maintain their own roles. Each role lists its purpose, its accountabilities, its domain (the decisions that belong to it alone) and the members who hold it. The [governance mode](/en/docs/governance-modes) decides who edits a role: any member in Free mode, the role's lead in Agile mode. In Strict mode, every change to a role goes through a proposal. Everyone sees a change as soon as it is saved, in every view of the org chart. The [history](/en/docs/history) records who made it and when, with a way to undo it. Rolebase has no HR, SCIM or directory connector. A developer can script changes through the [GraphQL API](/en/developers/graphql-api) if your HR data needs to flow in.

You can also run an HR system and Rolebase side by side, with contracts and reporting lines in the HR system and who does what in Rolebase.

## How to get started

This might seem like a lot to put in place, but the reality is that it can be quite simple.

1. You can start with exactly how your team is now:

- Each team becomes a circle
- Each sub-team becomes a sub-circle

2. Then, you can start filling out what you feel are the most important aspects of your business right now. Our users like to start with the accountabilities of the teams, sub-teams and roles, because the clarity they bring can change a lot. Again, you can go step by step. Start with:

- In the accountabilities section, list out the things that the teams, sub-teams and roles are currently doing that are recurring. Stick to the essentials. Just make sure the rest of the business knows what to expect from the different teams and roles
- Then you can move on to the KPIs for each team, sub-team and roles as this is tied to performance and needs to be visible for all to see
- The domains can be filled out in teams, sub-teams and roles where it's already clear what their decision-making perimeter is. For instance, in the tech team, you can put in the tech stack there. This means that only the tech team can ultimately decide on which tech stack they're using. They can still consult other teams before deciding. When it comes time to decide, though, they make the call.

As you see, you can start small and fill it out as the information gets clearer.

## Frequently asked questions

<FaqList>

<FaqItem question="How can I keep our company org chart up to date automatically as people change roles?">

Let the people whose roles change update the org chart themselves. Tools such as ChartHop, Pingboard or Organimi redraw reporting lines on their own when HR records a new manager or a new job title in the HR system or the employee directory. Responsibilities and decision rights change more often and never reach the HR system. In Rolebase, each team edits its own roles. The [governance mode](/en/docs/governance-modes) decides who may change what. You can keep your HR system for contracts and reporting lines and Rolebase for who does what. The two tools stay separate.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="How do I create a dynamic org chart that updates itself when the team changes?">

Build it from roles rather than from a drawing. Create one role per team and sub-team, invite the members, and pick a governance mode that lets each team edit its own roles, such as Agile mode. From then on, the people in each team update their roles. Every view of the org chart shows the change right away. For hires, departures and manager changes to reach the chart on their own, you need a tool synced with your HR system, such as ChartHop or OrgChart. Rolebase takes nothing from an HR system. It is free for up to 5 active members, so you can try it on one team first.
