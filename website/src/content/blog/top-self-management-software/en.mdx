Self-management software gives a team without a reporting line three things: a record of who holds which role, a way to change those roles together, and a log of the decisions. Eight tools do that seriously in 2026. They split into three groups: the full governance platforms that cover all three needs, the mapping tools that show who holds which role and leave the changes to you, and one decision tool that runs and records the decisions without holding any roles.

Rolebase publishes this article and is one of the eight. It comes first in the group of full governance platforms, ahead of GlassFrog, because it fits more than one method. Each entry links to the vendor's site so you can check every claim at the source.

Prices come from each vendor's own pricing page, read on 24 September 2026. Where a vendor publishes no price, the entry says so. Ratings come from Capterra with the review count beside the score. Four of these eight tools have fewer than 30 reviews there, Rolebase included, so read the count before the score. Two have no Capterra listing at all.

## How this ranking works

The groups come from how much of the governance cycle runs inside the tool. That cycle has three parts: the structure (roles, circles, their purpose and accountabilities), the process that changes it (a governance meeting, a proposal, a vote by consent), and the record of what was decided.

- Full governance platforms cover all three. A change to a role goes through the tool's process, then updates the structure.
- Mapping tools cover the structure. The people with editing rights change it after agreeing on the change in a meeting held outside the tool.
- Decision tools cover the process and the record. They keep no list of roles, so they record what was agreed without changing any role.

Inside the first group, three questions set the order, asked one after the other:

- Does the tool run the whole cycle, meetings and votes included? Fractale handles changes through written tensions and has announced voting for later, so it comes last in the group.
- Can you run governance and read the price before talking to a salesperson? Rolebase and GlassFrog put governance in a free plan with no time limit, Nestr publishes its price and offers a trial, and Talkspirit publishes neither a free plan nor a price, so it starts with a demo.
- Does the tool fit more than one method? This question settles the tie between Rolebase and GlassFrog. Rolebase supports several methods while GlassFrog follows Holacracy alone, so Rolebase comes first, even though GlassFrog's free plan covers twice as many users.

Inside the second group, the order comes from how much the tool does around the map.

The table and each entry also show the real entry cost with its seat minimums and paid add-ons, the licence and whether you can host the tool yourself, and the Capterra rating with its sample size.

The entries are numbered 1 to 8 across all three groups. Compare ranks only within a group, since each group serves a different need. A 12-person association that decides in a monthly meeting and only needs everyone to see who holds what can stop at the second group and pay almost nothing.

## Group one: the full governance platforms

These five hold the structure and the process that changes it. When members create, amend or remove a role through a proposal or a governance meeting, the tool records the change with its date.

They differ on the method they assume and on what you can see before paying. GlassFrog follows the Holacracy constitution to the letter, while the other four support several methods. Talkspirit adds a whole intranet around the governance. Fractale runs everything through tensions, with voting still to come.

### 1. Rolebase

![Rolebase homepage](../../../assets/screenshots/rolebase-homepage.png)

A role in [Rolebase](/en/features) carries a purpose, a domain, accountabilities and indicators. Roles nest into circles, one person holds as many as they need, and the org chart shows them as nested circles, a hierarchical tree or a photo directory. The source is published under the MIT licence, so you can host the product on your own servers.

Rolebase sets itself apart by letting each organisation choose its method and how strict its governance is. At setup, presets for Holacracy (versions 4 and 5 of its constitution) and for sociocracy install the base roles and the meeting templates each method expects. The organisation then picks one of [three governance modes](/en/docs/governance-modes). In Free, every member edits the org chart. In Agile, each role's lead edits that role. In Strict, every change to the structure goes through a [proposal](/en/docs/proposals). In a proposal, members prepare the org chart changes in a dedicated editor, then vote on the proposal by consent, unanimity, simple majority or absolute majority. The changes apply to the live org chart only once the proposal passes. Meetings follow the steps of reusable templates and end with a generated summary. Decisions, tasks and discussions with polls stay attached to the role they concern. A history keeps every change so you can undo it.

Two public cases show it in use. [Lonestone](/en/client-cases/lonestone), the 35-person product studio where Rolebase was first built, replaced its Notion tables with roles newcomers can read in their first week. The [Scop EVEA](/en/client-cases/scop-evea) cooperative keeps 140 people across Nantes, Lyon and Troyes on one org chart. Capterra rates Rolebase 5.0 from 16 reviews, and gave it Best Value and Best Ease of Use in 2023.

Rolebase keeps to roles, meetings, proposals and decisions. It has no OKR module, election feature, peer feedback, Slack integration or single sign-on, but a GraphQL API covers custom integrations and meetings sync with Google Calendar, Outlook or any iCal feed. Every plan includes everything Rolebase does, with no paid add-on. The free plan covers 5 active members, half as many as GlassFrog's, with every feature, proposals and meeting templates among them. People without an account appear in the org chart at no cost, so five account holders can map a whole organisation. Above that, [the Startup plan](/en/pricing) costs €5 per active user per month and includes one hour of coaching a month.

### 2. GlassFrog

![GlassFrog homepage](./glassfrog.png)

[GlassFrog](https://www.glassfrog.com) comes from HolacracyOne, the company behind the Holacracy constitution. The Holacracy website names it as its only support software. The product encodes that constitution: circles, roles, policies, linked roles, a tension inbox, and governance and tactical meetings run the way the method prescribes. It claims more than a thousand organisations, among them Siemens, Danone, Engie and ABN AMRO.

Its free plan has no time limit and the highest user cap among the tools in this group that also sell a paid plan. It covers 10 users with unlimited circles, roles and policies, both meeting types, projects and actions, and API access limited to 50 calls an hour. Premium costs $7 per user per month and adds custom meetings, full data download, SAML and integrations with Slack, Asana and Jira. Two add-ons sit outside that price at $1.50 per user per month each: Goals and Targets for OKRs, and FrogBot, an AI assistant that turns a tension into a governance proposal checked against your own policies. Enterprise terms start at 500 users.

GlassFrog suits a team that practises Holacracy by the book, since the tool already applies its rules. A team that practises sociocracy or its own mix has to learn the Holacracy vocabulary first. Capterra rates GlassFrog 4.3 from 60 reviews. A new version of its API went into public beta in May 2026.

### 3. Nestr

![Nestr homepage](./nestr.png)

[Nestr](https://nestr.io) was founded in the Netherlands in 2018 and hosts its data in Germany. It supports Holacracy, sociocracy, teal practices and custom governance. Its current focus is artificial intelligence: an AI agent can hold a role in the structure and take part in the work through MCP, and an assistant drafts a first structure from your website.

The Starter plan includes all of the governance: circles and roles, tensions, governance proposals, tactical and governance meetings, a playback of past governance changes, an audit trail and metrics. More than twenty connectors link Nestr to other tools. It imports existing structures from Holaspirit, GlassFrog, Peerdom or a CSV file. The Pro plan adds OKRs, peer feedback, SCIM provisioning and SAML.

Starter costs $6 per user per month on annual billing, and Pro $10. Prices appear in euros in some regions. The free Personal plan covers personal productivity for up to 5 people and includes no governance, so you need the 17-day trial or a paid plan to test governance. Nonprofits can ask for a reduced price. Capterra shows 5.0 from 2 reviews.

### 4. Talkspirit (formerly Holaspirit)

![Talkspirit homepage](./talkspirit.png)

[Talkspirit](https://www.talkspirit.com) has been a French collaboration platform since 2004. It merged with Holaspirit in 2023 and now sells one product it calls a European organisational operating system: governance, meetings, projects, OKRs, a news feed, chat, documents and an AI assistant, in one subscription. It claims more than 900 organisations and 150,000 users in 27 countries, hosts its data in France and Switzerland, and holds ISO 27001.

The governance model is the one Holaspirit built from 2016. Circles and roles carry a purpose, accountabilities, domains and policies. Meetings run check-in, agenda and closing rounds on templates the vendor draws from Holacracy and Sociocracy 3.0. Proposals, objections and elections run in the product. Talkspirit is the only tool here that sells governance and an intranet together, which suits organisations that want to replace their intranet at the same time.

The current Holaspirit platform closes on 16 October 2026. Its customers migrate from the Holaspirit administration page, with a button that carries over the structure and the history. The vendor states that the subscription continues with the same duration, price and scope. Talkspirit publishes no price, so you need a demo to get a number. Capterra rates the Holaspirit listing 4.6 from 122 reviews, the largest sample in this article.

### 5. Fractale

![Fractale homepage](./fractale.png)

[Fractale](https://fractale.co) is an open source platform released under AGPL-3.0, with its code on GitHub and commits in September 2026. It draws on Holacracy, sociocracy and teal organisations, and on the way developers work together on GitHub. It claims more than 500 organisations and names small studios, collectives and a festival among them.

Each circle and each role carries a mandate stating its purpose, its scope and what it can decide alone. Members raise tensions, structured threads sent to the right people. A tension can then lead to a change in a mandate or in the structure. What a member can see and edit depends on the roles they hold. A journal keeps every change. Kanban boards and projects attach the work to circles and roles.

Fractale is free to sign up to and publishes no paid plan. Voting by consent, a shared calendar and several integrations are announced as coming soon. The documentation still labels the platform beta. To host it yourself, you need to run Dgraph and Redis.

## Group two: the mapping tools

These two show the structure clearly and let the people with editing rights change it. You agree on the change in a meeting on your calendar or your video tool, so the mapping tool keeps the result without the proposal behind it.

### 6. Peerdom

![Peerdom homepage](./peerdom.png)

[Peerdom](https://peerdom.com) started in 2017 in Wabern, near Bern, inside the design agency Nothing, and became its own Swiss company at the end of 2021. It displays logos from Bayer, Greenpeace, Médecins sans Frontières, the Swiss Army and Mobiliar. It describes itself as independent of any single method.

Its map is the most flexible in this article, with circles, trees, pyramids and a 3D view, and a vocabulary you can adapt. Peerdom adds twelve optional apps to the map. With Drafts, you prepare a reorganisation beside the live structure and compare the two. With Contribution, you allocate a share of time to each role. Elections, peer feedback, goals, projects and analytics are available too. Directory sync with Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace and Okta keeps the list of people current. A GraphQL API, webhooks, Zapier and n8n cover the integrations. Data stays in Switzerland.

Peerdom is free up to 10 members. Peerdom+ costs 5 CHF per person per month on annual billing, with a 10-member minimum. Each app adds 1 CHF per person per month. The Pro plan is priced on request. Peerdom has no meeting module. Capterra rates it 4.8 from 8 reviews.

### 7. Maptio

![Maptio homepage](./maptio.png)

[Maptio](https://maptio.com) was created by Tom Nixon, a British author and coach who works on the source principle developed by the consultant Peter Koenig. It maps initiatives rather than positions: who has taken responsibility for what, drawn as nested circles, each with its lead and the people helping.

Maptio sticks to this map. You build the map, tag and filter it, and share it publicly or privately. Meetings, decisions and tasks stay in other tools. The code is open under AGPL-3.0 and free to host, though the last commit on the public repository dates from December 2025.

The hosted version runs on a pay-what-you-want model per organisation, with a suggested minimum of $10 a month and a recommended $50 to $200 for up to 300 people. A 30-day trial comes first. Maptio has no Capterra listing.

## Group three: the decision tool

### 8. Loomio

![Loomio homepage](./loomio.png)

[Loomio](https://www.loomio.com) has been built by a worker-owned cooperative in Aotearoa New Zealand since 2011. Its code is open under AGPL-3.0, with commits the week this article was written. It handles asynchronous decisions better than any other tool here.

A discussion leads to a proposal, which the group answers by consent, consensus, a vote or a sense check. Elections by consent follow the sociocratic process. Subgroups can stand for linked circles. Loomio also sends its notifications to Slack and Microsoft Teams. It holds no role definitions and no org chart, so it records what was agreed and leaves your structure as it is.

Loomio prices per organisation rather than per seat. Starter costs $399 a year, or $39 a month, for one group of up to 30 members, and Pro $999 a year for up to 3,000 members. Nonprofits pay $299 or $499 a year. Loomio has no free plan, only a 14-day trial for up to 10 people. Capterra rates it 4.8 from 26 reviews.

## Also considered

[teamdecoder](https://www.teamdecoder.com), from Karlsruhe, maps roles and workloads for teams that include AI agents. Beiersdorf and GLS use it. It publishes no price and is closer to team design than to governance. [logbuch.org](https://www.logbuch.org), from Winterthur, records sociocratic circles, roles and decisions and is free up to 10 members, with little visible activity since 2022. Some sociocratic groups draw their circles as relationship maps in [Kumu](https://kumu.io), where public projects are free and a private project costs $9 a month. [Decidim](https://decidim.org), from Barcelona, is built for participatory democracy in cities and large assemblies rather than for a team's roles.

## How to choose

Five questions narrow the list faster than a feature matrix.

### Which method do you practise?

A team running Holacracy by the book gets the most from GlassFrog, which the method's authors built. A team using consent the sociocratic way can vote by consent in Rolebase proposals, in Nestr governance proposals or in Loomio. Elections work differently from one tool to the next. Loomio, Peerdom and Talkspirit run the election itself in the product, while in Rolebase the election happens as a round in a meeting and the result is recorded as a decision. A team with its own mix of practices needs a tool that lets it set the rules: Rolebase through its three governance modes, Nestr and Peerdom through custom governance. If you are still deciding on the method, read how [holacracy and sociocracy differ](/en/blog/holacracy-vs-sociocracy) before you pick the software.

### Are you on Holaspirit today?

You have until 16 October 2026. Talkspirit keeps your structure, your history and your contract terms, and adds an intranet you may or may not want. Rolebase imports roles and members from a [Holaspirit export](/en/docs/import). Nestr imports Holaspirit structures too. Export your data before the closing date whatever you choose. We compared [the alternatives to Holaspirit](/en/blog/alternative-holaspirit) in more detail in a separate article.

### Should the meetings run in the tool?

If your governance meetings happen in a room with a facilitator and a shared document, a mapping tool is enough. Peerdom or Maptio will show everyone who holds what. If you want the agenda, the proposals and the decisions to update the roles directly, you need the first group. If your need is meetings outside governance, look at [meeting management software](/en/blog/best-meeting-management-software) instead.

### What will it cost at your size?

For a team of 20, Rolebase costs €100 a month and Nestr Starter $120 on annual billing. GlassFrog costs $140 a month before its add-ons, and Peerdom+ 100 CHF before its apps. Loomio costs $39 a month for one group of up to 30. Maptio asks what you want to pay for the whole organisation, from a suggested $10 to a recommended $50 to $200. Talkspirit gives a price only after a demo. Each vendor bills in its own currency, so the exchange rate can reverse the order of close amounts. Two plans set a limit: Peerdom bills at least 10 members, and Loomio's Starter plan covers a single group.

### Can you leave with your data?

Rolebase, Maptio, Fractale and Loomio publish their code, so you can host them yourself if the vendor changes course. The licences differ. MIT lets you modify and run the code as long as you keep its copyright notice, while AGPL-3.0 asks you to publish your changes if you offer a modified version to users over a network. For the other four, ask for the export formats during the trial or the demo.

If you already use Trello, Slack, Miro or Confluence, you can keep them [alongside a governance tool](/en/blog/horizontal-tools). We also compared Rolebase one to one with [GlassFrog](/en/blog/alternative-glassfrog), [Peerdom](/en/blog/alternative-peerdom), [Nestr](/en/blog/alternative-nestr) and [Loomio](/en/blog/alternative-loomio).

## Frequently asked questions

<FaqList>

<FaqItem question="What is self-management software?">

Self-management software records who holds which role in an organisation without a reporting line, gives the team a shared way to change those roles, and keeps a log of the decisions. It usually covers roles with a purpose and accountabilities, circles grouping those roles, and governance meetings or proposals.

It differs from an HR org chart, which shows positions and managers, and from a project tool, which tracks tasks without saying who has the authority to decide.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What software do Holacracy practitioners use?">

GlassFrog is the tool built by HolacracyOne, the company behind the Holacracy constitution. The Holacracy website names it as its support software. Its free plan covers 10 users with governance and tactical meetings.

Nestr, Talkspirit and Rolebase also run Holacracy-style governance. Rolebase does it through its Strict mode, where every change to the structure goes through a proposal.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Which tools support sociocracy and consent decisions?">

Loomio handles consent proposals and elections by consent in a sociocratic way, without a role structure. Rolebase lets a group vote on a proposal by consent, where it passes unless someone objects, and applies the prepared org chart changes once it passes. Nestr supports sociocratic governance through its governance proposals. Loomio, Talkspirit and Peerdom run elections in the product, while in Rolebase a team holds the election as a round in a meeting and records the result as a decision.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there free self-management software?">

Three tools let you run governance for free with no time limit. GlassFrog covers 10 users, meetings included. Rolebase covers 5 active members with every feature. People who need no account appear in its org chart for free. Fractale is free to sign up to in its hosted version and publishes no paid plan. Peerdom's free plan covers 10 members for the map, without meetings.

Rolebase, Maptio, Fractale and Loomio are also free if you host them yourself. On the hosted side, Nestr's free plan excludes governance and Loomio offers only a 14-day trial.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Is there open source self-management software?">

Four of the eight are open source. Rolebase is released under MIT. Maptio, Fractale and Loomio are released under AGPL-3.0. Rolebase and Loomio are the most complete of the four, Rolebase for roles and meetings, Loomio for decisions. Maptio focuses on the map, and Fractale on tensions and projects.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What happens to Holaspirit in 2026?">

The Holaspirit platform closes on 16 October 2026. Customers move to Talkspirit through a migration button in the Holaspirit administration, with their structure and history. Talkspirit states that the subscription continues at the same price, duration and scope.

Customers who prefer another tool should export their data before that date. Rolebase and Nestr both import Holaspirit structures.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Do you need software to practise holacracy or sociocracy?">

You can start without one. A team of eight can keep its roles in a shared document and its decisions in meeting notes. The software pays off when people hold several roles in several circles, when newcomers need to find who decides what without asking around, and when the decisions of last year need to be found again.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can Notion or Asana replace self-management software?">

Many teams start with them, since they can store the roles and track the tasks. They lack a process for changing a role, a vote by consent and a link between a decision and the structure it changes, so the roles written down there stop matching the decisions taken in meetings as the team grows. Lonestone, a 35-person studio, moved its roles out of Notion tables for that reason.

</FaqItem>

</FaqList>

## Where to start

Pick the group before the tool. If you only need everyone to see who holds what, Peerdom or Maptio will do it for little or nothing. If you decide mostly in writing and asynchronously, Loomio is the specialist. If you want a decision taken in a meeting to update the roles directly, look at the first group. GlassFrog and Rolebase let you try this for free, without a sales call.

Rolebase is [free for 5 active members](/en/pricing) with every feature, while the rest of the team appears in the org chart at no cost. That is enough to map your roles, run a few governance meetings and pass a first proposal by consent before anyone pays. You can [see every feature](/en/features) before you sign up.
