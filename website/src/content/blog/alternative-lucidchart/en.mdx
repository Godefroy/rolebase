Let us settle the obvious point first. If you are looking for an alternative to Lucidchart to draw flowcharts, database schemas, network diagrams or user journeys, Rolebase is the wrong answer. It has no drawing canvas, and that is a deliberate choice rather than an empty box on a roadmap.

What Rolebase replaces is narrower and far more annoying: the org chart someone drew by hand, that everyone praised for a week, and that went stale at the next reorganization. If that document is the reason you are shopping around, the rest of this article is for you.

## What Lucidchart does well

Lucidchart is one of the best diagram editors on the market, and it has earned that position. The canvas is fast, the shape libraries cover everything from AWS architectures to BPMN, and several people can edit the same document at once with their own cursor. Lucid claims 99% of the Fortune 500 among its customers, and the integrations with Microsoft 365, Confluence and Jira put a diagram exactly where the work already happens.

For org charts specifically, the tool goes further than a blank page. You import employee data from a CSV file, an Excel sheet or Google Sheets, and Lucidchart builds the chart for you. Smart shapes connect direct reports to their manager and keep the layout clean when you move someone. On the Enterprise plan, the import runs straight from BambooHR, and you can overlay HR fields such as department, hire date or pay rate on top of the boxes.

One clarification deserves a place here, since the question comes up in search results often enough. Nothing happened to Lucidchart. The product is alive and shipping, it simply lives under the Lucid brand now, alongside Lucidspark, and `lucidchart.com` redirects to `lucid.app`. We checked that on 1 August 2026.

## Why teams look for an alternative to Lucidchart

### The org chart ages the moment it is drawn

A Lucidchart org chart is a document. Someone owns the file, and that person becomes the bottleneck for every arrival, departure, team split and role handover. Data linking helps if your source spreadsheet is clean, and it still needs a manual refresh or a re-import to reflect what changed.

Scop EVEA, an environmental consulting cooperative, ran into exactly this before switching to Rolebase. The company went from 60 people in 2020 to 140 in 2023, spread across three offices, and its organization lived in a PowerPoint. Their communication manager described the situation better than we could: representing the company was "like exploring the meanders of an unknown river with a hand-drawn map". You can read [the full story of their migration](/en/client-cases/scop-evea).

### The free plan runs out fast

The free version of Lucidchart gives you 3 editable documents and 75 shapes per document. An org chart for a 40-person company blows through the shape budget on its own, and you have spent one of your three documents on it. That limit is a deliberate trial, and it makes the free plan a poor place to keep a document the whole company is supposed to consult.

### Large diagrams get heavy

Reviewers on Capterra regularly mention slowdowns once a document grows, and browser performance is the first thing to suffer. An org chart is one of the diagrams that grows the fastest, because it gains a box every time someone is hired.

### Premium shapes and features sit behind the tiers

Several shape libraries, Visio import and export, revision history and the Confluence, Jira and Microsoft 365 integrations are spread across the paid plans. Users also point at limited colour options and templates on the lower tiers. None of that is unreasonable for a diagramming tool, and it does mean the version you tested for free is not the version you will be paying for.

### A box is not a role

This is the structural limit, and no pricing tier fixes it. A rectangle holds a name and a title. It says nothing about what that person is accountable for, which decisions they can make alone, or the three other teams they contribute to. Every organization that distributes responsibility hits this wall, and the usual workaround is a second document listing who does what, which then drifts away from the first one.

## Where a drawing falls short of a real org chart

The gap shows up in four everyday situations.

Someone holds several roles across several teams. On a canvas you duplicate the box, and the duplicate is now a second source of truth that someone has to remember to update.

A team wants to see the same organization differently. Operations want a flat list of who does what, the executive team wants the nesting, a new joiner wants faces. On a canvas, that means three drawings, maintained three times.

A responsibility moves from one person to another. In a drawing you edit a label. In practice the accountabilities, the ongoing tasks and the meetings attached to that responsibility move too, and none of them live in the drawing.

Someone asks a question the chart should answer. Who can approve this expense? Who replaced Sarah on the recruitment role? The drawing shows a hierarchy, and the answer is somewhere else.

We wrote more about this shift in [the dynamic org chart](/en/blog/dynamic-org-chart) and in [how to create an org chart for your company](/en/blog/create-org-chart).

## How Rolebase approaches the org chart

### Every team maintains its own part

In Rolebase, each member updates their own roles, and each team updates its own structure. The org chart reflects reality because the people who know the reality are the ones editing it. Changes appear in real time for everyone, which matters most during a workshop where the structure is being reshaped live.

Protected governance is there for organizations that want a guardrail, and it restricts structural changes to circle leaders.

![Rolebase homepage showing the platform for autonomous teams](../../../assets/screenshots/rolebase-homepage.png)

### Roles carry their content

A role in Rolebase has a purpose, a domain and a list of accountabilities. It also carries its own discussions, tasks, decisions and meetings. Assign it to someone and they inherit the whole thing, which turns a handover into an assignment rather than a two-week archaeology project.

One person can hold five roles in four different teams without a single duplicate box, because the member and the role are two separate objects.

### Four views of the same data

The same organization renders four ways, and switching costs one click: All Roles for the full nesting, Holarchy for one team's roles at equal size, Operational for a flat view of everyone's roles mixed together, and Members only for the faces. Nobody maintains a second drawing to get a second view.

![Rolebase features page showing the platform's core capabilities](../../../assets/screenshots/rolebase-features.png)

### You still get an image when you need one

Slides and PDFs are a real need, and this is the one place where a diagram tool has an obvious edge. Rolebase exports the org chart as a transparent PNG, and you pick the view, the width in pixels and the light or dark rendering before downloading. It covers the board deck and the onboarding handbook. It does not give you a canvas to annotate afterwards.

Full organization data exports to XLSX or JSON, with a choice of what to include: members, roles, decisions, tasks, meetings.

### Open source, and free for small teams

Rolebase ships under the MIT license. The code is auditable, and you can run it on your own servers, which is usually the deciding factor when the org chart holds personal data and your DPO asks where it travels. The free plan covers every feature for up to 5 active members, with unlimited inactive members in the org chart, so a 40-person company can map its whole structure and give account access to the five people who maintain it.

## The other options worth a look

Recommending only ourselves would be convenient and not very useful.

**draw.io, also known as diagrams.net**, is the answer to "is there a free alternative to Lucidchart". It runs under the Apache 2.0 license, opens with no account, works offline as a desktop app, and imports Visio, Gliffy and Lucidchart files, which makes the migration nearly free. It builds a diagram from a CSV file and offers automatic layouts. It produces a drawing, with the same ageing problem, and it costs nothing. We compared it with five other options in [our roundup of open source org chart tools](/en/blog/open-source-org-chart-tools).

**Organimi** is a dedicated org chart builder aimed at HR teams. Pricing scales with the number of people charted rather than with seats, at $13 per month for the Basic plan and $25 for Premium at 150 people charted, billed annually. It imports CSV and Excel, connects to Active Directory, Google and Salesforce on the Premium plan, and handles matrix and accountability charts. It is a good fit for a classic hierarchy fed by an HR system.

**Microsoft Visio** is the answer to "what is the Microsoft equivalent of Lucidchart", and it makes sense for organizations already deep in the Microsoft stack. It is a diagram editor too, so the ageing problem follows you there.

If your organization practices shared governance and you are comparing tools in that specific category, our comparisons with [Peerdom](/en/blog/alternative-peerdom) and [GlassFrog](/en/blog/alternative-glassfrog) will be more useful than this one.

## Who should choose which

**Stay on Lucidchart if** your org chart is one diagram among fifty others, if your team draws architectures and processes every week, if you need the BambooHR sync, or if your organization is a stable hierarchy where one person legitimately owns the chart.

**Try Rolebase if** your org chart is redrawn after every reorganization, if people hold several roles across several teams, if you want each team to maintain its own part, if open source or self-hosting matters to you, or if you also need the meetings, tasks and decisions to hang off the roles instead of floating in a separate tool.

**Use both if** you draw in Lucidchart and organize in Rolebase. That is a common and perfectly reasonable setup, and the two tools are not competing for the same job.

## Migrating your org chart

Nobody rebuilds a 140-person structure in an evening, and nobody should try.

1. Export what you have. A Lucidchart org chart built from a data import means the source CSV or Excel file is still the cleanest starting point, so go back to it rather than to the drawing.
2. Create your organization in Rolebase and start with the teams, before the people. Five to ten circles is usually enough for a first pass.
3. Add the roles that already exist in practice, with a purpose and two or three accountabilities each. Resist the urge to be exhaustive on day one.
4. Invite the people who maintain the structure. Everyone else can appear as an inactive member, at no cost, until they need an account.
5. Run one meeting inside the tool. This is the step that decides whether the org chart stays alive, because a structure nobody uses in a meeting goes back to being a drawing.

Rolebase imports organizations from Holaspirit automatically. For any other source, upload your file and our team picks it up as a request rather than leaving you to retype everything.

Since the free plan has no time limit, running both tools side by side during the transition costs nothing.
